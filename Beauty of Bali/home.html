About Bali Culture

The Balinese ethnic group is the indigenous community of Bali Island. They are well known for strongly preserving their customs and traditions that have been passed down from generation to generation Balinese culture remains deeply rooted in daily life, from religious ceremonies to traditional arts, despite the influence of modern society. The majority of Balinese people practice Hinduism, and religious teachings strongly influence their customs and way of life.

Language

Balinese people use the Balinese language for daily communication within families and communities. The language has different speech levels depending on social status and respect toward others. Indonesian is also widely spoken because it is the national language. Because Bali is a popular tourist destination, many Balinese people can also speak English.

Traditional House

Traditional Balinese houses consist of several separate buildings arranged within one large compound. Each building has a specific function, such as sleeping quarters, guest areas, or family temples. Most homes also have a small shrine or temple used for daily offerings and worship. The entrance gate, called Angkul-angkul, often features unique carvings and traditional Balinese architecture.

Traditional Ceremonies

Ngaben

Ngaben is a cremation ceremony performed to honor the deceased. It is believed that cremation helps release the soul so it can continue its journey to the afterlife.

Galungan and Kuningan

Galungan and Kuningan are important Hindu celebrations that commemorate the victory of dharma (truth and goodness) over adharma (evil). During these celebrations, families visit temples and make offerings.

Nyepi

Nyepi is known as the Balinese Day of Silence. On this day, the entire island observes 24 hours of silence, meditation, and reflection. No travel, work, or entertainment activities are allowed.

Traditional Music

Gamelan

Gamelan is a traditional musical ensemble made up of gongs, drums, and metallophones. It produces fast, rhythmic music that accompanies dances and religious ceremonies.

Ceng-Ceng

Ceng-Ceng are small metal cymbals used to create sharp rhythmic sounds in gamelan music.
